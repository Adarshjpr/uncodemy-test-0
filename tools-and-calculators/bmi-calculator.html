What is BMI, and how is it calculated

Body Mass Index is a quick screening number that relates your weight to your height. It doesn't measure body fat directly, but it's a widely used starting point for gauging whether your weight falls in a healthy range.

The BMI formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

weight = your body weight in kilograms

height = your height in metres, squared

A worked example

A person who is 170 cm tall and weighs 65 kg works out like this:

Height170 cm (1.70 m)
Weight65 kg
Height squared2.89 m²
BMI≈ 22.5
CategoryNormal weight

A BMI of 22.5 sits comfortably inside the 18.5–24.9 normal range — being far outside this band on either side is linked to higher health risks.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI range?

For most adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered a normal, healthy weight range according to WHO guidelines.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

Not entirely. BMI doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes or very muscular people can show a higher BMI without excess body fat. It's also less reliable for elderly people, pregnant women, and growing children.

What should I do if my BMI is outside the normal range?

Treat it as a starting point, not a verdict. Talk to a doctor or dietitian who can look at your full health picture before making any changes.

Does BMI differ for men and women?

The formula is the same for everyone, but body composition naturally differs between men and women, so the same BMI can mean a different body fat percentage for each.
