How a discount is calculated

A percentage discount reduces the original price by that share of itself. Stack a second coupon and it applies to the already-discounted price, not the original — which is why two 20% coupons never add up to 40% off.

The discount formula

Final price = original price × (1 − discount ÷ 100)

Savings = original price − final price

For a stacked discount, run the formula twice — once per coupon, each time on the new price.

A worked example

A ₹2,000 item with a 25% discount, then a 10% coupon stacked on top:

Notice the two coupons together only save 32.5%, not 35% — because the second coupon works on the already-reduced ₹1,500, not the original ₹2,000.

Frequently asked questions

Do two discounts add up?

No. Stacked discounts multiply, not add. A 20% discount followed by a 10% coupon gives 28% off overall, not 30%, because the second discount is applied to the already-reduced price.

How do I work out the discount percentage myself?

Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide that by the original price, then multiply by 100. For example, ₹2,000 down to ₹1,500 is a ₹500 saving, which is 25% of ₹2,000.

Does this include tax?

No — this calculator works on the listed price only. If tax is added after the discount, apply it separately to the final price shown here.

What's the difference between markdown and discount?

They're usually the same thing — a percentage cut from the listed price. "Markdown" is more common in retail pricing, "discount" in coupons and promotions, but the maths is identical.

Shopping for something bigger?

Compare the sale price against a few stores before you commit — a bigger discount elsewhere can beat this one.
