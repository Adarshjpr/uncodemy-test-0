How percentages are calculated

A percentage is a fraction out of 100. Finding "X% of a number" means multiplying the number by X and dividing by 100. Percentage change compares a new value to an original one — and because it's always measured against the original, increases and decreases of the same absolute amount rarely produce mirror-image percentages.

The percentage formulas

X% of number = (X ÷ 100) × number

Percentage change = [(new value − original value) ÷ original value] × 100

A positive result means an increase; a negative result means a decrease.

Frequently asked questions

Why isn't a 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease back to the original?

Because each percentage is calculated on a different base. A 20% increase on 100 gives 120, but a 20% decrease is then calculated on 120, not the original 100 — giving 96, not 100.

How do I find what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second, then multiply by 100. For example, 40 out of 200 is (40 ÷ 200) × 100 = 20%.

What's the difference between percentage and percentage points?

A percentage point is a plain difference between two percentages, while a percentage change is relative. Going from 20% to 25% is a rise of 5 percentage points, but a 25% relative increase in the percentage itself.

Can percentage change be more than 100%?

Yes — if a value more than doubles, the percentage increase exceeds 100%. For example, going from 50 to 150 is a 200% increase.
