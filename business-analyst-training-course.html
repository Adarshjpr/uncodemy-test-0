What is a Business Analyst?

A Business Analyst (BA) is the professional responsible for identifying business needs, analyzing processes, and recommending solutions — often by interpreting data and acting as a liaison between stakeholders and the development team. BAs don’t just analyze problems — they define the right problems to solve.

In any project, the Business Analyst is the translator — converting business jargon into technical specifications and ensuring the final product or service aligns with the company’s goals. From gathering requirements and conducting stakeholder interviews to creating user stories, wireframes, and reports — the BA ensures what’s being built is exactly what’s needed.

1. What exactly does a Business Analyst do, and why is this role so critical in modern organizations?

A Business Analyst (BA) is the strategic link between business problems and technology solutions. The BA ensures that an organization doesn’t just implement systems and processes randomly, but rather designs those processes to align with business goals and user needs. This means they identify pain points in a business, gather and document requirements from stakeholders, analyze processes, and help suggest changes that drive real value. ...Whether it’s improving the customer onboarding process in a banking app, reducing churn in a subscription business, or defining new features in a SaaS product — BAs ensure the right problem is being solved the right way. They communicate with management, developers, QA teams, customers, and end users to gather insights, convert ideas into structured documentation (like BRDs or user stories), and ensure that business objectives are reflected accurately in technical outputs.
Without a business analyst, projects are often poorly scoped, solutions don’t match the actual need, or deadlines are missed due to miscommunication. Hence, the BA is considered the backbone of efficient decision-making and successful project delivery in every industry today. Read more
