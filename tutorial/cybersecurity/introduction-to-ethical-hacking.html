Introduction to Ethical Hacking

What Is Ethical Hacking?

Ethical hacking is the authorized practice of probing systems, networks, and applications for security weaknesses, using the same techniques as malicious attackers but with permission and the goal of improving security.

Ethical Hacking vs Malicious Hacking

The key distinction lies in authorization and intent - ethical hackers operate with explicit permission and a defined scope, and report findings so vulnerabilities can be fixed, whereas malicious hackers act without permission and for harmful or personal gain.

Types of Hackers

TypeDescription
White HatEthical hackers who work with authorization to improve security
Black HatMalicious hackers who exploit systems without permission
Grey HatOperate without full authorization but without malicious intent

Why Ethical Hacking Matters

By proactively identifying vulnerabilities before attackers do, ethical hacking helps organizations strengthen their defenses, meet compliance requirements, and reduce the likelihood of a damaging real-world breach.

Ethical hacking sets the mindset and legal boundaries for offensive security work, and that work is carried out through a structured process - the phases of penetration testing.
